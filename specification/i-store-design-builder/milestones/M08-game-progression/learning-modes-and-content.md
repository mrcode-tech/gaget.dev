# Learning Modes and Content

## Purpose

Define the beginner and advanced explanation modes and the content principles for learning material.

## Modes

### Beginner

- Prefer familiar, plain-language explanations.
- Explain design terminology when needed or requested.
- Use concrete examples tied to the current website activity.
- Focus on practical implications rather than professional jargon.

### Advanced

- Use concise explanations.
- Allow professional design terminology.
- Emphasize relevant design considerations and trade-offs.
- Avoid repeating elementary explanations unnecessarily.

## Shared behavior

- Both modes use the same journey, missions, catalogue, component capabilities, and composition rules.
- Modes change explanation depth and vocabulary only.
- They do not unlock editing capabilities, alter sample definitions, or change mission requirements.
- Users may change explanation mode without changing project composition or progress.
- No separate beginner/advanced product or project model is created.

## Content principles

- Keep explanations short and relevant.
- Prefer concrete examples over abstract lectures.
- Use synthetic content and generic placeholders.
- Do not require users to supply complete business content before progressing.
- Keep learning content contextual to the current mission.
- A separate learning library is not required in the initial scope.

## Implementation boundary

Beginner/advanced mode and the broader learning content are approved product direction, not part of M08's initial implementation scope, which is limited to progress overview and mission-status experience.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Beginner and advanced explanation characteristics are defined.
- [ ] Modes do not alter product capabilities or mission rules.
- [ ] Learning content is concise, practical, and contextual.
- [ ] Missing content does not block progress solely for learning purposes.
- [ ] A separate learning library is not required initially.
