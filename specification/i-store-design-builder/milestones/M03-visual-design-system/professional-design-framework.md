# M03 — Professional Design Framework

## Purpose

Establish the professional knowledge and practical skills required to design the builder and author its component portfolio. Apply experienced design judgment and recognized practices rather than generic templates or arbitrary visual decisions. This framework guides designers and engineers; it is not a user-facing score or gamification system.

## 1. Visual design fundamentals

Typography and hierarchy; color theory and contrast; composition, alignment, grids, spacing, proportion, and visual rhythm; balance, emphasis, density, and whitespace; image placement; and purposeful use of shape, borders, and elevation.

Apply by defining purpose and hierarchy before decoration and testing real content lengths, not only ideal placeholder copy.

## 2. UX and interaction design

User research and task analysis; information architecture and navigation; affordances, signifiers, feedback, error prevention/recovery; usability heuristics; interaction consistency; accessibility, keyboard interaction, focus, and assistive-technology semantics.

Apply by making sample preview, selection, insertion, and editing distinct; communicating shared versus breakpoint-specific changes; and preserving user control and reversibility.

## 3. Website patterns and composition

Navigation, hero, product/category displays, content, features, social proof, conversion, information, forms, footer patterns; page flow and visitor intent; patterns for commerce, portfolios, services, organizations, and other purposes.

Apply by defining the purpose/context of each sample and offering meaningful visual variety. Keep taxonomy generic while validating the first store journey.

## 4. Design-system engineering

Semantic tokens and themes; component anatomy, contracts, variants, slots, and data models; reuse boundaries, naming, documentation, versioning; separation of builder tokens from sample styling; stable IDs and compatibility.

Apply by reusing technical foundations without forcing identical visual styling and documenting shared, sample-specific, and breakpoint-specific behavior.

## 5. Frontend design engineering

Semantic HTML and component architecture; responsive CSS/layout; accessibility, performance, maintainability, browser behavior; isolation, state clarity, deterministic rendering; visual regression and interaction testing.

Apply by isolating the module from GaGet shell, implementing explicit sample contracts, and never exposing arbitrary CSS/low-level layout controls to end users.

## 6. Responsive design expertise

Content-driven breakpoints; adaptation of navigation, typography, imagery, grids, and actions; touch targets and mobile usability; device-specific ordering/visibility; representative viewport and intermediate-width testing.

Apply intentional desktop/tablet/mobile presentations while preserving the M02 three-canvas and desktop-originated shared-structure rules.

## 7. Design quality assurance

Heuristic review, task-based usability evaluation, visual consistency, alignment, hierarchy, spacing, accessibility, keyboard testing, responsive/content stress testing, visual regression, and acceptance evidence.

Review builder and samples against separate standards. Quality is a release-readiness checklist, not a subjective numerical score.

## 8. Design research and professional critique

Curated reference research, pattern analysis, product landscape observation, moodboards, visual direction, critique against intent/audience/hierarchy/usability/execution, iteration, and documented rationale.

Use references to understand principles, not copy proprietary layouts or branding. Record source, observed principle, intended adaptation, and original design decision. Prefer reputable professional, accessibility, and standards references.

## Practical review method

1. State user/task purpose and intended audience.
2. Identify the visual or interaction problem.
3. Research relevant patterns and constraints.
4. Define hierarchy, content assumptions, and responsive intent.
5. Create and inspect the design in realistic context.
6. Review usability, accessibility, consistency, and implementation feasibility.
7. Record rationale, decisions, issues, and follow-up improvements.

No AI capability is required for this framework or the initial product release.
