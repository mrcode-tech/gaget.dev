# Design Guidance Framework

## Purpose

Help non-designers understand component roles and make informed choices without taking creative control away from them.

## Guidance principles

- Use plain language and explain design terminology when necessary.
- Be concise and contextual; show more detail only when useful.
- Explain alternatives rather than naming a universal best choice.
- Distinguish factual capabilities from illustrative advice.
- Do not assume the user's industry, brand, content, or audience.
- Do not select, rank, or recommend a sample on the user's behalf.
- Keep guidance available independently of journey progression.

## Guidance content

### Component-type guidance

Explain:
- What the component is.
- What visitor need or website task it supports.
- Where it commonly appears.
- Which content it usually contains.
- Whether it is commonly optional or context-dependent.
- Related component types, where relevant.

### Sample-specific guidance

Explain:
- What distinguishes this sample's layout and hierarchy.
- What content it is designed to present.
- What its supported fields and finite options do.
- How its responsive presentations differ.
- What content or asset assumptions it makes.
- Situations where a user may find the approach useful.

Guidance must not claim that one sample is objectively superior or suitable for every website.

## Examples

Examples use synthetic content and are illustrative. They teach how a component or sample works; they do not rank options or imply that users should copy the example's business identity.

## Selection considerations

Offer neutral questions users may consider, such as:
- What should visitors notice first?
- Is the main purpose to explain, showcase, compare, or prompt an action?
- How much content is available?
- Does the design work with the user's likely content and assets?
- Is the mobile presentation suitable for the intended visitor task?

These are prompts for user judgment, not automated evaluation.

## Relationship to journey and missions

M01/M02 mission guidance may link to relevant categories or samples. The library remains fully accessible regardless of mission or level status. Guidance does not create mandatory component choices or override the user's explicit Not needed decisions.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Component types explain purpose and typical content.
- [ ] Samples explain their distinctive approach and constraints.
- [ ] Guidance uses plain language and synthetic examples.
- [ ] Selection considerations are neutral and non-prescriptive.
- [ ] No automatic recommendation, ranking, or selection is introduced.
- [ ] Library access is never gated by journey progression.
