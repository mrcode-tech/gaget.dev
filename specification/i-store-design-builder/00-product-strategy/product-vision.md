# Product Vision

## Vision

Help people without professional design skills create a clear, coherent website design by making their own design decisions through a structured, game-like journey.

## The problem

People often know what their business, organization, or project needs to communicate, but do not know how to turn that goal into a website structure. Traditional design software exposes too many technical controls, while website builders can hide important structural choices behind templates. Users need a way to understand the decisions and create a useful design without becoming designers or developers.

## The product

The Website Design Builder is a guided, interactive design workspace. It introduces website-design decisions as levels and missions, explains why each decision matters, and presents a library of high-level website components and patterns.

The user makes every creative choice: which components to include, which pattern to use, how to configure its available options, and how to order the page. The bounded website canvas is visible from the beginning and reflects those decisions immediately.

The product is game-like in its presentation and progression, but it is not a conventional game. It has no points, prizes, badges, leaderboards, timers, or competitive scoring. Levels organize the work and show completion; users can move between them freely.

## Initial target audience

A broad audience, initially optimized for:
- Small-business owners and entrepreneurs.
- Independent professionals and service providers.
- Creators, artists, and portfolio owners.
- Community groups and non-profit organizations.
- Organizations and teams needing an informational website.
- People exploring a website idea before working with a developer.

The product remains industry-neutral. These audiences are starting points, not restrictions.

## User's role

The user is the designer and source of creative decisions. They:
- Define the website's purpose, audience, and goals.
- Choose every page, section, component, pattern, and variant.
- Configure the options exposed by each selected pattern.
- Arrange, reorder, replace, and remove components.
- Supply their own text and other content when ready.
- Decide where images should appear, using placeholders or optional reference images.

The product must not automatically choose a design, generate content, or recommend a preferred design as if it were the user's decision.

## Product's role

The product:
- Breaks a complex design task into understandable levels.
- Explains design concepts and the purpose of high-level components.
- Presents a curated, browsable component and pattern library.
- Shows examples so users understand available choices.
- Applies the user's choices to the canvas.
- Tracks mission completion and preserves progress.
- Provides wireframe structures and clear placeholders.
- Keeps the project understandable to a developer at any stage.

## Initial output

The initial product creates a complete visual website wireframe/design when the user completes the journey. It does not create a production website or require final imagery.

A project can also be handed off before completion. A partial handoff must clearly show:
- The user's project purpose, audience, and intended outcome.
- Pages and sections already selected.
- Component patterns, configuration, and order.
- Text supplied by the user and content placeholders.
- Image placeholders and any optional reference assets.
- Decisions not yet made and work still outstanding.

A developer or later implementation workflow can use this information to understand what the user is trying to build.

## Product principles

1. **User agency:** the user makes all creative decisions.
2. **Guidance, not automation:** teach through levels, examples, and explanations; do not design on the user's behalf.
3. **Purpose before appearance:** begin with the user's intended audience and outcome.
4. **High-level choices:** use understandable website components and patterns, not technical layout controls.
5. **Visible progress:** keep the bounded canvas visible from the first workspace visit.
6. **Free revision:** allow users to revisit and change any previous decision.
7. **Wireframe first:** focus on structure, hierarchy, content intent, and image placement.
8. **Content ownership:** users provide their own content when ready; placeholders are valid.
9. **Industry neutrality:** adapt language and examples without hardcoding a business type.
10. **Useful partial work:** never discard or obscure the user's progress because a level is incomplete.
11. **Portable understanding:** preserve structured design decisions for developer handoff.

## Non-goals for the initial release

- AI-generated designs, copy, images, or AI recommendations.
- Automatic selection of components, layouts, or page order.
- A professional freeform design editor, infinite canvas, or arbitrary pixel placement.
- Requiring users to write CSS or understand grid, spacing, or breakpoint terminology.
- A conventional game economy, reward system, leaderboard, or score.
- A stock-photo library or requirement to provide final images.
- Production code generation, hosting, publishing, or domain management.
- Full CMS, ecommerce checkout, payment, booking, or membership backends.
